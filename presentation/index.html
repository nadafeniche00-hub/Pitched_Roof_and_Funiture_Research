How much of this attic can you stand in?

Under a pitched roof, usability changes across the room. Usable Space measures it, checks whether the furniture fits, and gives a floor-plan generator the feedback it is missing.

The pain

A generated plan can pass every rule and still not work.

A floor-plan generator turns a bubble diagram into rooms that satisfy sizes and adjacencies. Nobody checks whether a person can use the room, and under a roof that is where plans fail. Switch the lens.

    So the tool plays one role in a bigger system: it sits after the generator, scores every plan for usable height and furnishability, and returns reasons the generator can act on.

    The AI part

    Pretrained vision models, running on the university GPU, on real photos.

    Nothing is trained from scratch. Words go in, regions come out: Grounding DINO finds doors, windows and roofs, SAM 2.1 cuts the roof out, Depth Anything gives the depth, CLIP names the building and searches the photos by text.

    What this shows, and what it does not. The stack runs end to end on photos from the Real2Twin building (a stone church, not a house), zero-shot and without any fine-tuning. No accuracy is measured yet: that is the February work, with a hand-labelled set of roof-storey plans and roofs.

    Patterns from real roofs

    12,413 tagged buildings in Lippe tell the decision tree where to start.

    Not segmentation: counting. OpenStreetMap tags for buildings with a roof shape in Detmold's district, mined with pandas, scikit-learn and association rules. The result shapes the first question of the tree and shows what the engine does not cover yet.

    Roof shape of dwellings (8,332)

    What it changes in the tree

    Surveyed instead of tagged: gable pitch around Detmold (NRW LoD2, 11 tiles, 1,379 dwellings)

    Trust it for the shares, not for the details. Volunteered tags: the shape shares are plausible, but attic levels (96.2 % of gabled dwellings "have" one) and the material (100 % roof tiles) mostly show how mappers tag, and the roof angle is tagged on only 40 buildings. A simple depth-3 tree reaches 79.7 % against 68.4 % for always guessing "gabled", and nearly all of the gain is outbuildings being flat. February repeats this on surveyed LoD2 data.

    Every design choice in this project serves at least one stage, and every stage has at least one choice behind it. Switch choices off: a stage with nothing left turns red, and the loop it belonged to is cut.

      Generate and iterate

      A decision tree picks the pattern. A Swalloop-style loop builds it.

      Answer a few architectural questions and the pieces get anchors: tall things on the gable, low things under the slope. Then the loop places or moves one piece per pass and keeps only what raises the score.

      What came from Swalloop

      Run it

      A plan image goes in, a furnished and walkable attic comes out.

      These steps ran for real: step 2 on the university GPU server, steps 3 to 5 in the app. The plan is a synthetic test, not a real building.

      Plan

      Build, test against real rooms, make it physical.

      The prototype is the tool for exploring. The research is whether usable height and furnishability separate a roof storey that works from one that does not.

      Method

      1. Evaluate generated and real attic plans with the Usable-Space Index and the rules.
      2. Compare the verdict with architects' judgement on a small set of real roof storeys.
      3. Build scaled shading and movable-roof prototypes (SolidWorks, Bambu P1S) and compare measured light with the predicted sun hours.
      4. Document in the open: method, limits, data, and what did not work.
